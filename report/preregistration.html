OSF Secondary Data Preregistration: Inequality and Youth Protest Disposition around the globe

Author

Daniel Miranda & Nicolás Tobar

Published

August 19, 2026

1 Study Scope

This preregistration defines the confirmatory analysis plan for a secondary-data study using four repeated cross-sectional waves of ICCS/CivED (1999, 2009, 2016, 2022). The unit of observation is the individual student.

The substantive goal is to test whether country-level economic inequality is associated with students’ willingness to engage in protest behavior, with theory-informed controls at the individual level.

2 Question 1. What is the hypothesis being tested?

2.1 Primary contextual hypothesis

  • H1: Students in countries with higher inequality are expected to show higher protest disposition than students in countries with lower inequality, net of individual-level covariates and wave effects.

This is the primary confirmatory contextual hypothesis. The design is not treated as a balanced longitudinal panel, and therefore the confirmatory analysis does not rely on a within-country inequality effect as the main inferential target.

2.2 Individual-level hypotheses

  • H2: Gender is associated with protest disposition.
  • H3: Increase on civic knowledge is negatively associated with protest disposition.
  • H4: Having university-educated parents (parents_hisced) is associated with an increase on protest disposition.

3 Question 2. How will the key variables be operationalized?

3.1 Outcomes

Primary dependent variables are two ordered 4-category student items treated as approximately continuous in the primary model:

  • peaceful: willingness/disposition for peaceful protest.
  • trafic: willingness/disposition for blocking traffic.

Common question stem across waves (as used in project documentation and the INVALSI 2022 presentation):

  • There are many different ways how citizens may express their opinions about important issues in society. Would you take part in any of the following activities to express your opinion in the future?

Original wave-specific variables and item phrases for peaceful:

  • 1999 (CivED): BS5M9, label: PARTICIP.IN A NON-VIOLENT PROTEST MARCH.
  • 2009 (ICCS): IS2P31D, label: Peaceful march.
  • 2016 (ICCS): IS3G30C, label: Peaceful march.
  • 2022 (ICCS): IS4G31C, label: Peaceful march.

Original wave-specific variables and item phrases for trafic:

  • 1999 (CivED): BS5M11, label: BLOCK TRAFFIC AS A FORM OF PROTEST.
  • 2009 (ICCS): IS2P31H, label: Block traffic.
  • 2016 (ICCS): IS3G30J, label: Block traffic.
  • 2022 (ICCS): IS4G31L, label: Block traffic.

Original 4-category substantive response scale (all waves):

  • 1 = I would certainly not do this
  • 2 = I would probably not do this
  • 3 = I would probably do this
  • 4 = I would certainly do this

Wave-specific non-substantive codes (for example, 0 and 8 in CivED; 0/8/9/98/99 depending on wave files) are treated as missing according to the harmonization rules.

The primary estimator treats these outcomes as continuous; categorical/ordinal treatment is prespecified as robustness.

3.2 Contextual predictor (country-year)

  • gini: disposable-income Gini at country-year level.

The primary analysis uses a single country-year inequality measure, gini_ct, as the contextual predictor.

The primary contextual effect is therefore represented as a single coefficient on the country-year Gini value:

\[ \text{Gini}_{ct} \]

where \(c\) indexes countries and \(t\) indexes country-wave observations.

3.3 Individual covariates

  • gender
    • role: individual-level predictor.
    • measurement: binary.
    • original raw variables and labels:
      • 1999: BSGGEND/ITSEX, label includes GIRL OR BOY.
      • 2009: SGENDER, label Gender.
      • 2016: S_GENDER, label Gender.
      • 2022: SD_GENDER, label Gender.
    • original raw coding by wave before harmonization:
      • 1999: 1=girl, 2=boy.
      • 2009: 1=female, 0=male.
      • 2016: 2=female, 1=male (with fallback handling when country files use 0/1 coding).
      • 2022: 0=female, 1=male.
    • coding and reference: 1=female, 0=male (reference category = male).
    • missing handling: excluded under prespecified inclusion rules for complete design-consistent cases.
    • centering: none (binary indicator retained in original coding).
  • civic_test
    • role: individual-level predictor.
    • measurement: continuous harmonized civic knowledge score (PV1CIV/KNOWLMLE lineage).
    • original raw variables and labels:
      • 1999: KNOWLMLE/KNOCG_ML, label Civic knowledge score.
      • 2009: PV1CIV, label Plausible value civic knowledge.
      • 2016: PV1CIV, label Plausible value civic knowledge.
      • 2022: PV1CIV, label Plausible value civic knowledge.
    • raw version note: this is a test-score measure (plausible value/scale score), not a single questionnaire item with categorical response options.
    • coding and reference: numeric continuous, no categorical reference.
    • missing handling: excluded under prespecified inclusion rules for complete design-consistent cases.
    • centering/standardization: final rule frozen before model estimation and applied consistently across outcomes.
  • parents_hisced
    • role: individual-level predictor.
    • measurement: binary.
    • original raw variables and labels:
      • 1999: BSGEDUM and BSGEDUF, labels HOW FAR IN SCHOOL DID YOUR MOTHER GO and HOW FAR IN SCHOOL DID YOUR FATHER GO.
      • 2009: HISCED, label Highest parental education.
      • 2016: S_HISCED, label Highest parental education.
      • 2022: S_HISCED, label Highest parental education.
    • original raw version:
      • 1999: separate mother and father education categories, harmonized and combined using max(mother, father).
      • 2009, 2016, 2022: single highest-parental-education field.
    • coding and reference: 1=university parents, 0=non-university (reference category = non-university).
    • missing handling: excluded under prespecified inclusion rules for complete design-consistent cases.
    • centering: none (binary indicator retained in original coding).

3.4 Identifiers and design variables

  • idstudent, idcountry, study (wave), and country-wave context identifiers.
  • weights as harmonized student total weight in pooled data.
  • Replicate-weight, strata, and PSU variables will be taken from official wave-specific ICCS documentation and source files, then frozen in the analysis log before model estimation.

4 Question 3. What is the source of the data included in the analyses?

  • ICCS/CivED individual-level student data from IEA waves: 1999, 2009, 2016, 2022.
  • Contextual inequality data from SWIID (country-year Gini).
  • Harmonized analysis dataset from the project processing pipeline (pooled student-level file) linked to country-year Gini.
  • Project repository for analyses and processing pipeline: nicolastobarj/gini-iccs.

5 Question 4. How will these data be obtained?

  • ICCS/CivED data are obtained from official IEA repository webpages:
    • CivED 1999: https://www.iea.nl/data-tools/repository/cived-1999
    • ICCS 2009: https://www.iea.nl/data-tools/repository/iccs-2009
    • ICCS 2016: https://www.iea.nl/data-tools/repository/iccs-2016
    • ICCS 2022: https://www.iea.nl/data-tools/repository/iccs-2022
  • SWIID data are obtained from the SWIID webpage: https://fsolt.org/swiid/
  • Data integration is reproducible through the project processing scripts and documented country-name harmonization rules.
  • Gini assignment follows a prespecified country-year matching rule; no retrospective matching based on substantive results is allowed.

6 Question 5. Are there any exclusion criteria for the data?

6.1 Observation-level inclusion

Primary analysis includes all student observations with non-missing:

  • outcome (peaceful or trafic, depending on model);
  • country-year Gini value;
  • prespecified individual covariates;
  • required design information (weights and any required variance-estimation fields).

6.2 Exclusion principles

  • Countries are not excluded because they are absent in some waves.
  • Country-wave nonparticipation is treated as structural design imbalance, not ordinary missingness.
  • Countries observed in only one wave remain eligible for the primary model; they contribute to between-country estimation but limited within-country change information.

7 Question 6. What statistical analyses will be performed?

7.1 Data structure

The study is repeated cross-sectional multilevel data:

Student -> Country x Wave.

Students are not followed longitudinally across waves. Country participation across waves is unbalanced.

7.2 Primary specification

For each outcome (peaceful and trafic), estimate a multilevel model with:

  • fixed effects: country-year Gini, gender, civic_test, parents_hisced, and wave fixed effects;
  • random effect: country random intercept;
  • wave represented as categorical fixed effects for 1999, 2009, 2016, 2022;
  • country identification based on idcountry and wave based on study.

The primary contextual predictor is the observed country-year Gini score, not a within-country decomposition. The main model therefore estimates a general inequality effect while accounting for the unbalanced cross-national structure.

Conceptual model:

\[ Y_{ict} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{Gini}_{ct} + X_{ict}\beta + \gamma_t + u_c + \varepsilon_{ict} \]

where \(Y_{ict}\) is the protest disposition outcome for student \(i\) in country \(c\) and wave \(t\), \(\text{Gini}_{ct}\) is the country-year Gini value, \(X_{ict}\) includes gender, civic_test, and parents_hisced, \(\gamma_t\) is the wave fixed effect, and \(u_c\) is the country-level random intercept.

This specification is the confirmatory model.

7.3 Model constraints

  • Do not treat the within-country decomposition.
  • Do not replace wave fixed effects with a random year intercept.
  • Do not specify year as a level above country.
  • Keep gini as the single primary contextual term in the main model.

7.4 Centering and scaling

  • The main contextual predictor is the observed country-year Gini value.
  • Individual covariates will be prespecified as coded in the harmonized file; any standardization decision will be fixed before estimation and applied consistently across outcomes.

7.5 Complex survey design integration

Primary estimation must respect ICCS complex sampling design. Before substantive estimation, the analysis will audit official ICCS technical documentation by wave to identify:

  • student sampling weight;
  • school/institution sampling information;
  • strata and PSU definitions;
  • replicate weights/replication method, if provided;
  • wave-specific variance-estimation requirements.

The final estimator will prioritize official IEA guidance for variance estimation. If no single estimator can jointly represent all required multilevel and survey-design components, the methodological trade-off will be documented and an appropriate alternative estimator will be included as sensitivity analysis.

7.6 Software

All analyses will be implemented in R. Package selection will be finalized only after confirming support for multilevel random effects and ICCS design-based inference requirements.

8 Question 7. What are the criteria for confirming or disconfirming the hypotheses?

  • Two-sided tests with alpha = 0.05.
  • 95% confidence intervals for all focal coefficients.
  • Primary evidence will be based on sign, magnitude, and uncertainty of:
    • the country-year Gini coefficient;
    • prespecified individual-covariate coefficients.
  • Interpretation will not rely on p-values alone.
  • Effective contextual information is determined by country-wave variation, not by student count alone.

9 Question 8. Have the analyses been previously validated on a subset of the data?

The project has prior data-processing and harmonization checks, including pooled-data construction and descriptive validation. These prior checks are acknowledged as preprocessing validation and not treated as confirmatory hypothesis tests.

A previous presentation on INVALSI 2022 was used as project background, prior analyses were conducted on pre-2022 IEA waves (1999, 2009, 2016), and a simpler modeling strategy was used for prediction of pacific actions. Those earlier analyses did not implement the current full preregistered specification and excluded 2022 data.

No additional model tuning based on outcome-specific confirmatory results will be performed prior to preregistered model execution.

10 Question 9. What is already known about the data that could be relevant to the hypotheses?

  • The country-wave panel is unbalanced because ICCS participation differs by wave.
  • Contextual Gini varies at country-year level; contextual effective sample size is country-wave based.
  • Harmonization across waves requires explicit mapping of item names/coding and country identifiers.
  • Complex survey design features differ by wave and require official documentation review before final estimation settings are frozen.

11 Question 10. Provide a brief timeline for the different stages of the preregistration.

  1. Complete wave-by-wave ICCS design audit (weights, strata, PSU, replication method).
  2. Lock primary estimator and design-based variance procedure in R.
  3. Build analysis-ready modeling dataset with the single country-year Gini predictor and fixed wave effects.
  4. Run preregistered primary models for peaceful and trafic.
  5. Run preregistered robustness analyses, including any exploratory within-between decomposition as a sensitivity check only.
  6. Compile and report results with explicit confirmatory versus robustness labeling.

12 Prespecified Missing-Data Rules

  • Missing outcome: observation excluded from the corresponding outcome model.
  • Missing Gini or Gini components: excluded from primary model.
  • Missing individual covariates: handled by prespecified complete-case rule unless design-consistent imputation is preregistered before estimation.
  • Missing weights/design variables: observation excluded from design-based primary estimator.

If multiple imputation is adopted, it will be prespecified before estimation with explicit handling of clustering and survey-design variables.

13 Prespecified Robustness Analyses

A. Restricted participation sample: countries observed in at least two waves.

B. Secondary model with a within-between decomposition of Gini as a sensitivity analysis, interpreted as exploratory unless the design structure supports it.

C. Categorical sensitivity model for ordinal outcomes.

14 Country-Wave Gini Matching Protocol

  • Source: SWIID country-year Gini series used by project pipeline.
  • Harmonization: prespecified country identifier/name crosswalk.
  • Year rule: assign Gini by the ICCS observation year for each country-wave.
  • Missing Gini handling: prespecified exclusion or justified temporal rule; no post hoc choice based on results.
  • Boundary/name differences: handled through documented mapping rules.
  • Any interpolation or nearest-year substitution must be explicitly justified and declared before model estimation.
